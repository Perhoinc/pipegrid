WHAT THIS IS DECIDING

The job you set: see whether the whole stack is connected inlet to outlet all the way through, and read what a junction needs. So the model is:

Flow starts at every inlet with nothing attached — that is where it enters the build. It crosses a joint only where an outlet meets an inlet. It ends at every outlet with nothing attached. Anything it never reaches is the thing you are looking for.

Every panel below shows the same little build: flow in at the left, a run, an elbow, a split, one branch ending properly in the open, and one branch with two outlets butted together — which is not a joint, so everything past it is dead. That dead tail is the case the whole mode exists to find.

Not here, by your call: no rising/falling markers (you can see that in 3D), nothing on the 2D sheet, and no tapping to flip a part — port configuration is the part, so flipping one would quietly change what you have to cast.

WHERE FLOW STOPS
two different things, and they should not look the same

An open outlet is a finished end — it is where the piece pours out, and it should look deliberate. A joint that does not join is a mistake. Do you want the mistake to shout (red, as drawn), or to stay in the same amber vocabulary and simply be marked differently? Red is the app’s refusal colour, so it would read as “this is wrong” rather than “this is the end”.

AND ONE THING I WANT TO CHECK

A stack of any size will have a lot of pipe moving at once. On the biggest board you have built, every lit pipe animating together may read as noise rather than as flow — which would argue for treatment D, or for the motion running only on the run you last touched.

Worth deciding on this board: does everything move at once, or only what you point at? I can make the whole stack move and let you tell me if it is too much, but it is cheaper to decide here.
