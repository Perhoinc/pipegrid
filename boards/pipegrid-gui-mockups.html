PIPEGRID — GUI TREATMENTS
three layout directions for the vertical slice's chrome, same scene and same 39-shape catalogue in each
The current vertical slice's controls were built as plumbing to prove the mechanics (pose system, gizmo, connect-drag, save/load) — never designed as something to actually use. This board is a real design pass on the chrome around that proven 3D scene, not a change to any mechanic. The scene itself, the port/pipe rendering, and every gesture stay exactly as they are today — what's under comparison is only: where the shape picker lives and how it handles 39 shapes without becoming a wall of tiny buttons, where camera/status readouts go, how board actions (undo/reset/save/load) are reached, and how the 2D reference view gets accessed. Tap the shape-picker control or the dock/side panel entries in each frame below — they're live enough to show the actual open/close behaviour, not just a static screenshot.

A · OVERLAY HUD

3D canvas gets the whole screen, edge to edge — closest to "3D is the app." Controls float on top and mostly stay out of the way until touched. Tapping the current-shape pill slides up a full sheet for picking a different one, tier by tier. Tradeoff: the floating buttons sit directly over the scene, so at some camera angles they could obscure a part underneath them — would need testing on the real device to see if that's ever actually a problem in practice.

A fixed tool panel, always visible, nothing to remember to open — closer to a conventional CAD app. The 3D canvas gives up a real, permanent strip of width for it though (shown here at roughly a third of the frame). Tradeoff: on an iPad in portrait this panel would need to become a bottom strip instead of a side strip, which changes its shape and how much fits per row — worth deciding whether that reflow should be automatic or the panel should just get harder to use in portrait specifically.

A native-iPadOS-style tab bar: five clearly-labelled destinations, each opening its own popover above the dock rather than a full sheet. Reads the most like "a real app" of the three, at the cost of a permanent (if slim) 58px strip that isn't scene. Tradeoff: five icons is already a full dock — there's no obvious sixth slot left if something else needs top-level billing later (e.g. a trace/flow mode, if that ever gets ported from the 2D app).
