THE GIZMO
Three rings, one per grid axis. Drag a ring to turn the part about that axis — it snaps in quarter turns, because those are the only orientations that exist. The rings are the pose system's own generators, so this control physically cannot produce an invalid pose: every quarter turn lands on one of the 24. Drag the arrows to shift the part a whole cell. Drag anywhere else to orbit the camera — that's your viewpoint moving, not the part. Rings stay locked to the grid's axes rather than tumbling with the part, so a given ring always means the same real-world axis.

DECISIONS BAKED IN HERE — OVERRULE ANY OF THEM
  • Quarter-turn snapping, not free spin. Only 24 orientations exist, so continuous rotation would mean nothing. The part jumps as you drag rather than following your finger smoothly.
  • Rings are world-aligned. They stay on the grid's axes instead of tumbling with the part, so the same ring always means the same real axis. The alternative (rings follow the part) is the other common CAD convention — worth trying if this feels wrong.
  • Ring colour sits outside the mark grammar. Pipe-coloured means structure and amber means flow; these rings mean neither, which is why they get their own colours. The argument is that a gizmo is transient UI, not part of the drawing. The PALETTE button up top swaps between classic CAD red/green/blue and a quieter set tuned to the app — judge which one belongs in a drawing-office instrument.
  • The far half of each ring is dimmed so you can read which way it faces in depth. Rings seen edge-on stay grabbable — the hit area doesn't collapse with the drawing.
  • Translation is whole cells only, along one axis at a time, matching how the grid works. No free positioning.
