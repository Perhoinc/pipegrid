HOW SHOULD THE APP ASK FOR SOMETHING?
prompt treatments · three alternatives, the same three moments in each · 1 Oct
WHAT THIS IS DECIDING

The app asks for things in small grey text in the top-left corner, on the line under the camera readout. That is a status line — it carries az, el and zoom — and an instruction put in the same place reads as more status. Which is why “tap a cell, then PASTE” gets missed.

Every string below is one the app really produces. The three moments are the three kinds of thing it ever says: it wants something from you, it is in the middle of something, and it refused. Treatment A is exactly what ships today.

Three questions to settle, and they are independent — you can take the placement from one and the colours from another: what the colours mean, whether the tile carries the way out, and what happens to the corner readout. The last two panels take those separately.

The trouble is that the colours are spoken for. Green and blue are the N–S and U–D axes, on the compass in the same corner and on every gimbal ring. The blue-grey is the wireframe. A tile in axis green, beside a compass with axis green in it, teaches the wrong thing. It also asks you to learn five colours to be told something a word already says.

DOES THE TILE CARRY THE WAY OUT?
Without
az 35° · el 25°
transformtap the cell to turn 2 parts from

You leave the way you came in — tap TRANSFORM again in the dock. Nothing new to learn, but the exit is at the bottom of the screen while the instruction is at the top.

With
az 35° · el 25°
transformtap the cell to turn 2 parts from×

The thing that says what is happening also ends it. One more touch target up there, and it has to be big enough for a thumb without crowding the words.

AND THE CORNER READOUT?
Keep it, trimmed
az 35° · el 25° · zoom 100%
pastetap a cell, then PASTE

The corner goes back to being only what it is good at: where the camera is. Everything the app wants from you moves to the tile. This is the one I would take — and it is the one that makes the az/el control on the roadmap land somewhere sensible, since that readout becomes the thing you tap for it.

Replace it
pastetap a cell, then PASTE

Cleanest stage, but the camera numbers go with it — and you asked for a control to type exact angles into, which needs somewhere to live.

Both tiles sit over the top of the scene. On a tall iPad that is empty sky; on a short landscape window it is not, so whichever wins should be checked there — and in the light theme, where amber on pale paper needs more care than amber on black.

One constraint the board found on its own: the tile has to fit between the camera chip and the compass, and in the narrow panels above it does not — the transform sentence runs straight into the compass until it is capped. So whichever treatment wins, the prompts have to be written short. “tap the cell to move and turn 2 parts from” is already too long for a phone in landscape; “tap a cell to turn from” is not. Worth deciding on this board, because it changes the words, not just the box they sit in.
