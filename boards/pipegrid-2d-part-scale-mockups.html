WHAT THIS IS DECIDING

The plan draws a part's geometry at half the proportion it has in the 3D view. A port sits 42% of the way from the cell centre to the cell edge here, where in 3D it sits at 84%. That is why the parts look small in large cells, and it is the same number for every option below — only the multiplier changes.

It also makes multi-cell runs wrong, not just small. A 3-cell straight occupies three cells but is drawn at half that length, so the pipe stops short of the cells it actually fills. Watch the long run on the left of each panel: at ×0.50 it reaches barely past the middle cell; at ×1.00 it reaches its own far cell, the way the 3D view draws it.

Line weight and port size scale with the part in each option, so each one is internally consistent. Option A is exactly what the sheet draws today. Every panel below has the identical layer in it: a flat elbow, a T, a corner with an upward port, runs of 3, 2 and 1 cells, and one pipe passing vertically through the floor.

MY READ

D, matching the 3D view. Not mainly for looks: it is the only one where a part is drawn across the cells it genuinely occupies, so a 3-cell straight on the sheet is as long as the three cells it fills. It also means the plan and the 3D view agree about how much of a cell a fitting takes up, which matters when you are reading one against the other.
