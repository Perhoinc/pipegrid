All 24 orientation-preserving rotations of a cube, built by repeatedly combining two basic moves (spin around vertical, tip forward) until nothing new turns up. If this table isn't all green, nothing downstream can be trusted.
2. HAND-DERIVED SPOT CHECKS — ALL NINE CONNECTIVITY PATTERNS
Every number in this table was worked out by hand first and only then compared against what the code produces — that's what makes it a test rather than a readout. Assigning a flow role raises a shape's pose count or leaves it equal, never lowers it: a role removes symmetry, and fewer self-matching rotations means more orientations are genuinely tellable apart. Bare rows have no flow role at all (silhouette only, never actually placed on a board) — they exist purely to check the rotation math against shapes simple enough to reason about unaided. Their three simplest counts land exactly on the cube's own features: straight 3 (its 3 axes), elbow 12 (its 12 edges), corner 8 (its 8 vertices) — not a coincidence, since a two-adjacent-port shape is an edge and a three-perpendicular-port shape is a vertex.
